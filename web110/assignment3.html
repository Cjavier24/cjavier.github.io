As a web design aspirant, It's extremely important to understand how web search engines work. It doesn't matter how nice my page looks or how useful the information on it is if nobody can find my website, right? First, as the activity indicated, I searched the same keyword in different web search engines to compare the results. I searched the word "Apple"; Google, Brave, and Bing showed me MacBooks, iPhones and other Apple products instead of the fruit. However, the results changed in the image window: Brave mostly gave me images of the fruit, while Bing showed pictures of the company. Google, on the other hand, gave me both images of the fruit and the company. As a personal note, even though I prefer using Brave, I have to admit Google usually offers better results every time I search for pictures online. Second, I tried using Boolean searches. I already knew these filters existed, but I never sat down and tried them. The only one I knew how to use was "before:Date". I know it because if you enter "before:2023" in your search, it won't show any AI generated content, which is interesting to me. I still decided to try the other filters, like "filetype:pdf". I chose to search "The Wizard of Oz", and the first result without the Boolean search showed me articles about the movie, but after typing the specific filetype, it gave me pdf's with summaries of the story and even the whole book with all the pages. I'll use this feature whenever I need very specific information on a topic. Third, I had no idea what the difference was between AI and machine learning. These concepts can sound similar, so it's helpful to know that AI mimics human intelligence, while machine learning learns patterns from data. The video's explanation from IBM Technology makes it easy to understand, honestly. Finally, I read the article titled "Environmental impact of AI". It's truly concerning how much energy and water AI data centers need to work properly. I was personally unaware of the E-waste AI produces. I really thought this topic would be similar to the last one we saw about web browsers, but I was wrong! I learned a lot about how web search engines work, and I will definitely use this knowledge to my advantage.
