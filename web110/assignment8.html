Cybersecurity is one of the biggest concerns online. Someone who doesn't know how to stay safe online might have a hard time, since they could easily download malware by accident. However, even experienced users can be in danger if they don't understand how Internet safety actually works. To begin with, I didn't know there were different kinds of website cookies; I always assumed they were all the same. This is one example of a simple feature that everyone knows, but they aren't fully aware of. That's why it is important to stay informed about cybersecurity. The first activity was to read two articles about Internet safety. They explain how cybercriminals attack and how to stay safe. They also touched on encryption, which I knew at a surface level. Encryption is a fundamental part of cybersecurity and internet safety; understanding these ideas is essential. As a web designer/developer, I prioritize users' safety above all else. The second activity consisted of reviewing browser privacy settings. My main browser, Brave, wasn't included in the list, so I had to do my own research. Brave's privacy settings block third-party cookies by default, as well as pop-ups and redirects. Sites can use JavaScript and show images by default, but I can easily change these settings in Brave's settings. Websites are allowed to ask for access to my location, camera, microphone, or notifications, but cannot access them without my authorization. Brave also lets me see permissions and data stored across sites I've visited. Thanks to this research, I can configure my settings as much as I want to protect my privacy. Last, I tested my firewall. Windows 11 has its own antivirus system built right into the operating system. I checked for updates and did a quick scan that took less than a minute. It scanned 29.930 files on my computer, and everything was safe. Windows Defender usually updates automatically, but now I know it's not harmful to do a quick check sometimes. As a web designer/developer, safety is first; if my computer is infected with malware, the attackers could access my websites and, therefore, anyone who visits them, so this information is crucial. In conclusion, Internet safety should always be a priority. I learned a lot about encryption, browser privacy settings, and my operating system. This valuable information will keep me safe, as well as every user who visits the websites that I plan to build.
