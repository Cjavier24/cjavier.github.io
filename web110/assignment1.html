Assignment 1

I always thought I had a great knowledge of the fundamentals of the Internet. However, I was surprised by how much I was missing.

First of all, I wasn't especially interested in the history of the Internet until today. I had no idea that the person who invented the Internet didn't make it for the reasons we all use it today. The concept of a "communication system" that would survive a nuclear attack made sense back then, but I never would have guessed that was the initial purpose of developing the Internet.

On the other hand, I had the chance to play around with GitHub once again. I used it a few years ago for homework; unfortunately, most of my memories about this website are gone, but I am glad I can give it another try. GitHub is really powerful, and employers are more likely to consider you if you know about it.

I also discovered several useful shortcuts, such as Windows + Arrow to quickly move a window to one side of the screen (much faster than dragging it manually), which can help with multitasking. Some of these shortcuts are well known, for example, CTRL + Left Click to select multiple files separately. However, I didn't know this concept could also be applied to things like text in a document. I immediately tried all the shortcuts shown in the video as soon as I saw them, and I can confidently say that, as somebody who's studying to become a web designer, they will be indispensable and save me a lot of time.

Finally, I found out multiple key terms that everyone who works related to the Internet should know, like "cloud computing", "IETF," or "TIA". These terms will eventually come up in any conversation, so it's a good idea to memorize them.

In summary, I've absorbed many concepts about the Internet that I wasn't aware of. There's still a lot to learn.
